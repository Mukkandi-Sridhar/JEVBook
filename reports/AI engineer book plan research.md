# Plan the Book Around Decisions, Not Frameworks

Plan the book as a path built on judgement and light on any one framework. It should run from probability to production agents and follow one Indian running project from start to finish. Sridhar Mukkandi's decision layer from *Decide, Don't Generate* (calibration, cost-based thresholds, act/review/escalate, cheap "System One" models) should be the spine that ties every part together. This gap is open. The current canon either predates agents (Chip Huyen's *AI Engineering* gives agents one chapter and predates MCP and A2A), catalogues patterns (Gulli, Lakshmanan and Hapke), or chases a protocol that changes within months (an MCP book published on 20 October 2026 already had to note that the 2026-07-28 spec rewrote the protocol). The field has also moved. Model architectures have converged. Most of the 2025–2026 action is in context engineering, the stateless MCP rewrite, A2A joining MCP under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, RL from verifiable rewards behind "thinking" models, and a shift in security from "bad outputs" to "bad actions": Excessive Agency rose to #3 in OWASP's 2026 LLM list. Hiring managers screen for the same skills that production teams lack. They want candidates who have run evals, shipped beyond a notebook, and can justify every number in a project. Practitioners report that quality, not cost, is the top barrier. The famous failures (Replit, PocketOS, EchoLeak, Air Canada) came from system design, not model intelligence. The recommended structure has ten parts and about 34 chapters. It changes the author's order in three ways: evals come early, an agent loop is built from the raw API before any framework, and security is introduced together with tool calling. Framework code lives in a versioned repo, not on the printed page. Scope is the biggest risk, because the full list of topics goes well beyond the 350–530 pages typical of rival titles. Many late-2026 model names, dates and figures in the research are unverified and are listed at the end for checking before print.

### Foundations: statistics for evals matters more than maths for models

Practitioner curricula agree that applied AI engineers can start building without a formal ML background. Chip Huyen's companion repo says only that "a rough understanding of how AI works under the hood is useful to prevent misuse." Its theory reading is short: CS231n notes, Karpathy's "Zero to Hero", and Murphy as an optional "intense" reference ([chiphuyen/aie-book](https://github.com/chiphuyen/aie-book/blob/main/resources.md)). Her book places theory inside applied chapters (the transformer in Chapter 2, embeddings in Chapter 3, backprop in Chapter 7) and gives evaluation two full chapters ([aie-book](https://github.com/chiphuyen/aie-book/blob/main/resources.md)). That ratio is the right signal for Sridhar's book.

The must-have list is a synthesis, not a quoted curriculum. No survey measures which foundations working AI engineers actually use. On the statistics side it covers sampling and distributions, which explain temperature and top-p; conditional probability, which frames a model as P(next token | context); log-probabilities and softmax; cross-entropy and perplexity; confidence intervals and the bootstrap for eval scores; paired comparisons, including why n=50 is noisy; precision, recall and calibration; and base rates for guardrail classifiers. Classical ML should cover splits and leakage, and treat logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees as strong, cheap baselines, which is exactly where "System One" models fit. It should also cover embeddings, cosine similarity and k-NN as the basis of vector search. Deep learning needs loss, gradient descent, backprop at a conceptual level, the split between parameters, activations and optimiser state (needed for memory maths), and mixed precision. SVM theory, VC dimension, graphical models and HMM/CRF pipelines can be skipped or treated as history ([foundations synthesis drawing on aie-book](https://github.com/chiphuyen/aie-book/blob/main/resources.md)).

### Transformers and LLMs: one standard block, many deltas to save KV cache

The core decoder-only transformer has barely changed. A Korean commentary on Sebastian Raschka's 2026 LLM Architecture Gallery is titled (translated) "LLM architectures are converging; the real war is elsewhere" ([wikidocs](https://wikidocs.net/blog/@jaehong/9574/)). The book should teach one "standard 2026 block" and present everything else as deltas from it. That block is pre-norm RMSNorm, a SwiGLU MLP or MoE, RoPE, and GQA or MLA. Nearly every 2025–2026 attention change exists to shrink the KV cache. Raschka's March 2026 visual guide traces the path MHA → GQA → MLA (DeepSeek V3, Kimi K2, GLM-5, Mistral Large 3) → DeepSeek Sparse Attention → hybrid linear-attention or Mamba layers (Gated DeltaNet, Nemotron) ([Ahead of AI, snippet only](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/visual-attention-variants)). One teaching point prevents a common confusion: FlashAttention is an exact kernel optimisation, while sparse and linear attention change the computation ([FA3, arXiv 2407.08608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08608)). Engineers should learn to estimate KV-cache memory as layers × KV heads × head dimension × 2 × bytes × tokens. At high concurrency and long context, the KV cache, not the weights, often limits GPU capacity.

**Mixture-of-experts is now the default for large open models**: Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B, DeepSeek V4 at 1.6T total and about 49B active, and a Qwen3.8 flagship at 2.4T-A95B ([Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/blog/open-source-llms); [Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/open-source-llms-landscape-2026/)). Dense models persist at small and medium sizes (Gemma 4 up to 31B, Qwen 27B). The misconception to fix is that memory scales with *total* parameters while per-token compute scales with *active* parameters. DeepSeek V4-Flash is cheap to compute but still needs memory for all 284B parameters. DeepSeek V4 (24 April 2026, 1M context) is the flagship example of 2026 efficiency design, using a compressed hybrid attention (CSA + HCA). **Its reported 27% of FLOPs and 10% of KV cache relative to V3.2, its architecture details and its licence (MIT vs Apache 2.0, sources conflict) are UNVERIFIED**, because the technical report was not read ([smol.ai](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-04-24-deepseek-v4); [datanorth.ai](https://datanorth.ai/news/deepseek-releases-v4-open-source-1m-context)).

Post-training has moved along the line SFT → RLHF/PPO (InstructGPT) → DPO → **RL from verifiable rewards with GRPO-family algorithms**, which 2026 preprints describe as the dominant approach for reasoning ([PaperGym, arXiv 2608.31119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31119); [DeepSeek-R1, arXiv 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)). GRPO drops the learned critic and uses the group-average reward as its baseline ([arXiv 2603.01162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01162)). Refinements such as DAPO, GSPO and Dr. GRPO address length bias and instability ([Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/reasoning-rl-in-2026)). Applied engineers need this only at the conceptual level. It explains why models are strongest at maths and code (where rewards can be checked), why reasoning traces are long and costly, and why vendors now sell "reasoning effort" knobs. Test-time compute (Snell et al.) means the book should teach **cost per completed task, not price per token** ([arXiv 2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)).

For serving, self-hosting in 2026 means vLLM, SGLang or TensorRT-LLM. These combine a paged KV cache, continuous batching, prefix caching (SGLang's RadixAttention), and speculative decoding, where EAGLE-3 is described as "the de facto industry standard" ([SpecForge, arXiv 2603.18567](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567v1)). vLLM shipped EAGLE 3.1 on 26 May 2026 and speculative decoding on AMD GPUs on 23 August 2026 ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-05-26-eagle-3-1); [vLLM AMD](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-08-23-speculative-decoding-amd-gpus)). Speculative decoding is not free: adversarial prompts can collapse acceptance rates ([arXiv 2607.21804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21804)). Quantisation guidance is stable. GGUF Q4_K_M is the local default, Q5_K_M and Q6_K are near BF16, AWQ is usually the best 4-bit choice, FP8 is near-lossless on Hopper and Blackwell, and NVFP4/MXFP4 (about 4.5 bits per value) need validation block by block ([fungies.io](https://fungies.io/llm-quantization-gguf-awq-gptq-guide-2026/); [traeai](https://learn.traeai.com/t/ai-engineering/phases/17-infrastructure-and-production/09-production-quantization)).

The book should also correct three internal misconceptions that cause production bugs. First, temperature 0 is not deterministic in serving, mainly because batch size changes the kernel reduction order. Secondary summaries of Thinking Machines' work report 80 unique completions in 1,000 runs on default vLLM, against 1 with batch-invariant kernels ([batch-invariant explainer](https://mohakchugh.is-a.dev/blog/batch-invariant-kernels-deterministic-llm-inference)). Second, a 1M-token window does not mean the model uses 1M tokens reliably ("context rot", below). Third, "open weights" does not mean a permissive licence: Qwen3.8's flagship is revenue-gated and Llama 4 has a custom community licence ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/open-source-llms-landscape-2026/); [xigh/open-weight-models](https://github.com/xigh/open-weight-models)).

On model families, print should describe **tiers and selection criteria, not version numbers**. The tiers are frontier reasoning; a fast, cheap workhorse; small on-device models; and large open MoE models. Early-2026 releases are well corroborated: GPT-5.2 on 11 December 2025, GPT-5.3-Codex on 5 February 2026, and Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Sonnet 4.6 in February 2026 ([Wikipedia GPT-5.2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.2); [TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/frontier-ai-models-february-2026)). **The September–October 2026 frontier names (GPT-6 Astra/Sol/Luna, GPT-6.1, Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 5.5, "Fable"/"Mythos 5.1", Gemini 4 Argon, Gemini 3.8 Flash) come only from aggregators that disagree with each other and are UNVERIFIED** ([LLM Gateway timeline](https://llmgateway.io/timeline); [Microcenter](https://www.microcenter.com/site/mc-news/article/this-week-in-ai-oct-2-2026.aspx)).

### Prompting has become context engineering

Anthropic's "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (29 September 2025) set the new frame. Context engineering means "curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens during inference". The goal is "the smallest set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of your desired outcome", within a finite "attention budget". System prompts should sit at the "right altitude", between brittle if-else rules and vague guidance. Tools should be minimal and non-overlapping. Data is retrieved "just in time" through tools ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). For long horizons the post recommends compaction, tool-result clearing, structured notes and sub-agents that return summaries of about 1,000–2,000 tokens. The evidence behind this is Chroma's "Context Rot" report (July 2025). Across 18 models, accuracy fell as inputs grew, even on trivial tasks and well inside the advertised window, and distractors made it worse. The primary report was unreachable, so **figures come from secondary summaries** ([Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/context-rot); [DAIR.AI](https://academy.dair.ai/papers/context-rot)).

Reasoning models changed prompting habits. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 guidance says to describe outcomes and success criteria rather than steps, to move schemas into Structured Outputs, to use the Responses API so reasoning items carry across turns, and to start at `medium` effort ([OpenAI GPT-5.5 guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model/gpt-5.5.md), summarised via search). "Think step by step" is now mostly redundant. Self-consistency survives as "sample N, then verify or vote" for high-stakes outputs. **Prompt caching is now a design constraint.** Caches match only byte-identical prefixes, so static content must come first. Manus calls KV-cache hit rate "the single most important metric" for a production agent, with input-to-output token ratios of about 100:1 ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)). Structured outputs reached maturity in this period. Anthropic's constrained-decoding JSON outputs and `strict: true` tool use began as a November 2025 beta and are now GA. They support a subset of JSON Schema ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). GPT-5 added custom tools constrained by a context-free grammar ([OpenAI GPT-5 guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model/gpt-5.md)). vLLM supports free-form reasoning with a constrained final answer ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.9.1/examples/online_serving/openai_chat_completion_structured_outputs_with_reasoning.html)).

### Retrieval: hybrid search plus reranking is the dull winner, and agents now grep

The reliable 2026 RAG stack is good parsing, **hybrid BM25 plus dense retrieval fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion** (k=60 is a common start), metadata filters, a reranker over the top 20–50 candidates, and evaluation-driven iteration ([SemEval-2026 Task 8, arXiv 2606.28352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28352); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hybrid-search-rag-vector-keyword-reranking-2026)). Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval prepends a 50–100-token context to each chunk. It cut top-20 retrieval failures by **49%, and by 67% with reranking**. The same post advises skipping RAG altogether for knowledge bases under about 200K tokens ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Rerankers are not automatic wins: the ARAGOG benchmark reportedly found no notable gain from Cohere Rerank ([arXiv 2407.13193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13193)). Full GraphRAG is expensive. Microsoft's LazyGraphRAG indexes at 0.1% of GraphRAG's cost and matches its global-query quality at more than 700x lower query cost ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)). A vendor's "91% vs 67% multi-hop" claim for GraphRAG has no traceable source and should not be used ([Delos](https://delos.so/blog/graphrag-vs-vector-search-enterprise-knowledge-retrieval)).

The biggest shift is **agentic search**. Inside agents, retrieval has become a set of tools (grep, glob, search, SQL) that the model calls in a loop. Jason Liu reported grep beating embeddings in Augment's SWE-bench agent, and Doug Turnbull wrote in April 2026 that agentic search is "having a grep moment" ([jxnl.co](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/09/11/why-grep-beat-embeddings-in-our-swe-bench-agent-lessons-from-augment/); [softwaredoug.com](https://softwaredoug.com/blog/2026/04/06/agentic-search-is-having-a-grep-moment)). Cursor still combines custom embeddings with grep, so claims that coding agents have dropped vector DBs entirely are overstated ([Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/agentic-search)). Multimodal RAG now uses late-interaction vision retrievers (ColQwen3 and ColQwen3.5, supported in vLLM) that embed PDF pages as images ([vLLM ColQwen3](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/api/vllm/model_executor/models/colqwen3/)). MTEB leaderboard figures from 2026 blogs conflict, so embedding choice should rest on a 100–500-query recall@k eval on the reader's own corpus, with Matryoshka truncation as a cost lever ([Ailog](https://app.ailog.fr/en/blog/news/embedding-models-2026)). For vector stores the consensus is pgvector by default, Pinecone for zero-ops, Qdrant for filter-heavy self-hosting, turbopuffer for huge multi-tenant corpora on object storage, and LanceDB for embedded use. Several guides add that the embedding model, chunking and reranking matter more than the database ([Encore](https://encore.dev/articles/best-vector-databases); [Cognee](https://www.cognee.ai/best-vector-database)). For RAG evaluation, measure retrieval (recall@k, MRR) and generation (faithfulness) separately. One 2026 pipeline showed context recall of 0.04–0.23 alongside faithfulness of about 0.7, which is a clear example of high faithfulness hiding poor retrieval ([CUBO, arXiv 2602.03731](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03731)).

### Protocols: MCP went stateless and A2A joined it under one roof

MCP is the de facto agent-to-tool standard. Its **2026-07-28 specification is a major rewrite** ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)):

- **Stateless core.** The `initialize` handshake and `Mcp-Session-Id` are retired. Each request carries its version, identity and capabilities in `_meta`, so any server instance behind a load balancer can answer it.
- **Header routing.** `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers let gateways route requests without parsing the body.
- **Multi Round-Trip Requests.** These replace server-initiated sampling and elicitation.
- **Extensions framework.** Tasks, MCP Apps and Enterprise Managed Authorization are now formal extensions.
- **Deprecations.** Roots, Sampling, Logging, the legacy SSE transport and Dynamic Client Registration (replaced by Client ID Metadata Documents) are deprecated, under a formal 12-month deprecation window.

The SDKs see "close to half-a-billion downloads a month" ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)). Independent snapshots put the registry at about 30,000 servers by September 2026 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc)). Any book written against the 2025 specs (sessions, sampling, SSE) is now out of date.

Governance has consolidated. The Linux Foundation formed the **Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) on 9 December 2025** with MCP, goose and AGENTS.md ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2025/12/agentic-ai-foundation/)). Google's A2A, announced in April 2025 and donated to the Linux Foundation in June 2025, **moved into the AAIF in August 2026** ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/08/17/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-open-ai-standards)). The A2A v1.0 date conflicts (January vs March 2026). Practitioners describe A2A as widely endorsed but far less deployed than MCP ([glukhov.org](https://www.glukhov.org/ai-systems/comparisons/a2a-protocol-2026-adoption/)). The wider protocol map for a chapter is:

| Connection | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Agent to tool | MCP |
| Agent to agent | A2A |
| Agent to front-end | AG-UI and MCP Apps |
| Agent to payments | OpenAI/Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol and Google's AP2 |
| Procedural knowledge | Agent Skills, an open standard since 18 December 2025 ([unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-opens-agent-skills-standard-continuing-its-pattern-of-building-industry-infrastructure/)) |
| Repository instructions | AGENTS.md |

The book should warn that "ACP" names three different protocols. MCP's security record is poor. Secondary sources report 30+ CVEs in about 60 days in early 2026, around 43% of them shell or command injection, and **individual CVE numbers and CVSS scores should be checked on NVD** ([heyuan110](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-03-10-mcp-security-2026/)).

### Frameworks: stable at 1.0 and still changing

LangChain and LangGraph reached 1.0 on 22 October 2025. `create_agent` replaced `create_react_agent`, and middleware brings built-in human-in-the-loop, summarisation and PII redaction ([LangChain blog](https://blog.langchain.com/langchain-langgraph-1dot0)). The OpenAI Agents SDK and Responses API (March 2025) took over from Swarm and Assistants, and the **Assistants API sunset was set for 26 August 2026** (not confirmed as completed) ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/assistants-api-beta-deprecation-august-26-2026-sunset/1354666)). The Claude Agent SDK (renamed 29 September 2025) exposes Claude Code's harness: compaction, subagents, hooks, MCP and Skills ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-agents-with-the-claude-agent-sdk)). **Microsoft Agent Framework merged AutoGen and Semantic Kernel.** It reached a release candidate on 19 February 2026 and reportedly 1.0 on 3 April 2026 (GA date from secondary sources only). AutoGen is now in maintenance mode ([MS devblogs](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/agent-framework/migrate-your-semantic-kernel-and-autogen-projects-to-microsoft-agent-framework-release-candidate/)). CrewAI, LlamaIndex Workflows, DSPy (prompt optimisation against a metric), Pydantic AI (type-safe; v1.0 September 2025 from training knowledge), smolagents, Mastra and the Vercel AI SDK fill niches. **Their current versions were not re-verified.**

Anthropic's advice still frames the debate: frameworks "add layers of abstraction that can obscure the underlying prompts and responses"; start with the APIs, and "if you do use a framework, ensure you understand the underlying code" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). The Assistants-to-Responses migration and the retirement of AutoGen are good cautionary tales about API churn. The book should therefore teach a taxonomy. Orchestration runtimes are LangGraph, Microsoft Agent Framework and LlamaIndex Workflows. Vendor harnesses are the OpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Agent SDK and Google ADK. Role-play multi-agent frameworks are CrewAI and the AutoGen lineage. Optimisation and typing layers are DSPy and Pydantic AI. UI toolkits are Vercel AI SDK and AG-UI. The practical 2026 default is a thin SDK, MCP for tools, and the reader's own control flow.

### Agent architectures: the autonomy ladder and "parallel readers, single writer"

Anthropic's "Building effective agents" (19 December 2024) still sets the vocabulary. Its building block is the augmented LLM. It defines five workflows (prompt chaining, routing, parallelisation, orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimiser) and separates them from autonomous agents, which run "LLMs dynamically direct[ing] their own processes", with "higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). ReAct, Reflexion and plan-and-execute are now best taught as *behaviours of reasoning models inside a harness*, not as prompt templates. Long-running harnesses follow a recognisable pattern. A progress file and git history carry state across context windows. Each session makes incremental progress and runs end-to-end tests before marking work done, which guards against the failure of declaring victory too early ([dev.to summary of Anthropic harness post](https://dev.to/claudiuspapirus/why-ai-agents-fail-long-projects-and-the-anthropic-fix-3029)).

The multi-agent debate has settled. Anthropic's research system beat single-agent Opus by 90.2% on an internal eval, but at about **15x the tokens of chat** (single agents use about 4x) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" showed parallel subagents making conflicting decisions. Its April 2026 follow-up concluded that multi-agent setups work when "writes stay single-threaded" ([Cognition 2026](https://cognition.ai/blog/multi-agents-working)). The MAST taxonomy found 14 failure modes across 1,642 traces, with reported failure rates of 41–87% ([dev.to](https://dev.to/tuomo_pisama/do-you-actually-need-a-multi-agent-system-3a3j); [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)). The default to teach is one agent, one loop, a well-designed tool interface, context management and human checkpoints. Readers should escalate to orchestrator plus subagents only for breadth-first research, context isolation or best-of-N voting. For specialised agents, coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Copilot agent, Cursor) and computer-use agents are mainstream. **The 2026 browser-agent churn (Atlas retirement, Project Mariner shutdown, "ChatGPT Work"), the OSWorld 2.0 figure of 20.6%, the JetBrains coding-agent market shares and a claimed SpaceX acquisition of Cursor are all UNVERIFIED single-source or secondary claims. The acquisition claim must not be printed without a primary source** ([codercops](https://blog.codercops.com/blog/ai-browser-agents-comet-atlas-mariner-2026); [ajayk.sh](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/coding-agents-market-2026-share-productivity/)).

### Security: from bad words to bad actions

The **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026** (early August 2026) keeps Prompt Injection at #1. It moves **Excessive Agency from #6 to #3**, the biggest change on the list, and replaces System Prompt Leakage with "Hidden Context Exposure" ([Aembit](https://aembit.io/blog/the-owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-2026-what-changed-and-why-it-matters/); [Check Point](https://blog.checkpoint.com/ai-security/reading-the-signals-in-the-owasp-llm-top-10-2026/amp/)). An arXiv robustness study finds the Excessive Agency rise robust, but says the lower-ranked entries are not statistically separable ([arXiv 2608.19266](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19266)). OWASP also published a separate **Top 10 for Agentic Applications** on 9 December 2025, covering goal hijack, tool misuse, identity abuse, memory poisoning, cascading failures and related risks ([Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/)). Simon Willison's **"lethal trifecta"** (private data, untrusted content, an exfiltration channel) is the standard frame. His position is that the reliable fix is to remove one leg, usually exfiltration ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/willison-names-lethal-trifecta-behind-ai-agent-data-leaks)). Architectural defences are now published. The six patterns paper (action-selector, plan-then-execute, dual-LLM and others) applies one rule: untrusted input must not trigger consequential actions ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/13/prompt-injection-design-patterns/)). DeepMind's CaMeL (SaTML 2026) tracks data flow with capabilities ([IEEE CSDL](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/satml/2026/785900a587/2jZks3ksGT6)). Classifiers such as Llama Guard 4, Prompt Guard 2, NeMo Guardrails and Guardrails AI are defence in depth, not the security boundary ([Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/ai-guardrails-compared-nemo-guardrails-ai-llama-guard)).

For regulation, the EU's Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) moved Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)). For Indian readers, the DPDP Rules (notified November 2025) phase in substantive consent, notice and breach obligations around May 2027 ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). MeitY's AI Governance Guidelines are principles-based and not binding ([AI Policy Tracker](https://aipolicytracker.org/jurisdictions/india)). Observability tools that capture prompts create a second copy of personal data, so PII redaction before prompts *and* before traces belongs in the guardrails chapter.

### Evaluation and observability: error analysis first, trajectories over answers

The practitioner consensus, led by Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar, runs in this order:

1. Read real traces.
2. Open-code the failures and group them into a taxonomy.
3. Write cheap code checks for each failure mode.
4. Use LLM judges only for subjective modes, with binary pass/fail labels, after aligning the judge to a domain expert's critiques ("critique shadowing").

([AI News summary](https://buttondown.com/ainews/archive/ainews-creating-a-llm-as-a-judge); [Maven lesson](https://maven.com/p/0c0359/error-analysis-the-ai-engineer-s-best-roi)).

Judges show position, verbosity and self-preference bias, so readers should report agreement with human labels (TPR/TNR), not raw accuracy ([DEV](https://dev.to/anukumar/llm-evaluation-evals-that-catch-regressions-and-when-to-trust-llm-as-a-judge-1mmn)). This is the author's calibration material applied to evaluators. Agents need evaluation at three levels: end-to-end, trajectory and component. Final environment state must be checked too. In AgentRewardBench, a judge passed a "buy cereal" task when the agent had only added the item to the cart ([arXiv 2504.08942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08942); [arXiv 2603.15483](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15483)). Sierra's pass^k metric is the canonical demonstration that "works once" is not "works reliably". In the retail domain, GPT-4o-class success fell below about 25% at k=8 ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents)).

On tooling, ClickHouse acquired Langfuse on 16 January 2026, and Langfuse stays MIT-licensed ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/16/database-maker-clickhouse-raises-400m-acquires-ai-observability-startup-langfuse/)). LangSmith fits LangChain users, Phoenix is the open-source self-hosted option, and Braintrust and Weave compete as well ([Arize, vendor-biased](https://arize.com/resources/llm-and-agent-evaluation-platforms/)). The OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions remain at "Development" status, so attribute names may change ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/opentelemetry-genai-semantic-conventions)). Teach OTel-portable tracing concepts rather than one vendor's interface.

### Fine-tuning, LLMOps, cost, small models and multimodal

**Fine-tune last, for behaviour, not knowledge.** Use LoRA or QLoRA SFT on an open model (Unsloth, TRL, Axolotl), then DPO if you have preference pairs, and GRPO only when a verifier exists ([FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/llm-fine-tuning-techniques-i-ii/)). Thinking Machines' Tinker (1 October 2025) offers managed LoRA-only SFT and RL on open models from 1B to over 1T parameters ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/announcing-tinker/)). **OpenAI's fine-tuning platform is reported to be closed to new users. This was seen only in a search snippet of the RFT guide, so the wording and date are UNVERIFIED** ([OpenAI RFT guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reinforcement-fine-tuning)). Distillation (frontier outputs, filtered by evals, used to train a small LoRA model) is the most practical path for high-volume narrow tasks. Note that this rests on general practice, not a cited 2026 source.

For cost and latency, the reliable wins in order are:

1. Prompt caching (cached reads at about 10% of input price on Anthropic).
2. Batch APIs (about 50% off).
3. Routing or cascades (a reported 40–70% saving on mixed workloads).
4. Discipline on output length and reasoning effort.

Semantic caching reaches only about 25–35% hit rates and carries a risk of wrong answers ([DevToolLab](https://devtoollab.com/blog/prompt-caching-guide); [Kalvium Labs](https://www.kalviumlabs.ai/blog/model-cost-optimization-cutting-llm-bills/); [KunalGanglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/reduce-llm-api-costs-production)). Gateways (LiteLLM, Portkey, Helicone, Kong, Bifrost) handle fallbacks on 429 and 5xx errors, budgets and per-team keys ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/ai-gateway-litellm-portkey-kong-gpu-cloud/)). The local sweet spot is 27–35B models (Qwen 27B dense, Qwen 35B-A3B, Gemma 4 31B) at 4-bit on one GPU ([Computing for Geeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/open-source-llm-comparison/)).

**Coverage is thin for small/local tooling (MLX, Ollama, LM Studio, Phi) and almost absent for multimodal and voice (vision encoders, realtime speech APIs).** Disaggregated prefill/decode serving and tokeniser trends were not researched either. The research notes leave these as gaps, so those chapters need their own research pass before drafting.

## Hiring managers want evals, shipped systems and defended numbers

The role definition is stable. swyx's "The Rise of the AI Engineer" (June 2023) is credited with naming it. The ML engineer's job "stops when they serve inference" and the AI engineer's starts there, along a dotted line ([GitNation](https://gitnation.com/contents/the-rise-of-the-ai-engineer)). swyx suggested about four AI engineers for every ML engineer ([Humanloop](https://humanloop.com/blog/what-is-an-AI-Engineer)). In India the same role appears as "GenAI Engineer" or "Python GenAI Engineer" at services firms ([NodeFlair](https://nodeflair.com/jobs/infosys-python-genai-engineer-503324)). In US postings, Python appears in 62–71% of AI engineering roles, cloud in 55% and foundation models in 51%. **Only 2.5% of roles target 0–2 years of experience** ([Axial Search](https://axialsearch.com/insights/ai-engineering-jobs); [365 Data Science](https://365datascience.com/career-advice/career-guides/ai-engineer-job-outlook-2025/)). That last figure matters for the book's beginners: freshers usually enter through SWE roles or services-firm GenAI tracks. Indian postings stress Python, LLM apps, RAG, vector DBs, MLOps and backend skills. Infosys interviews probe RAG vs fine-tuning, reranking, FastAPI concurrency and database indexing ([Interview Query](https://www.interviewquery.com/guides/infosys-ai-engineer)).

Interview loops come in two shapes. Product companies and startups run a recruiter screen, coding, an LLM concepts round, AI system design (chatbot, RAG, agent), a take-home or practical project, and behavioural rounds ([Nora](https://interview.norahq.com/interview-guides/applied-ai-engineer-interview-questions-guide-2026)). One 2026 startup guide asks candidates to write an evaluation harness over model outputs and warns against hiring someone who has "only called the API" ([Recruiting From Scratch](https://www.recruitingfromscratch.com/blog/how-to-hire-an-llm-ai-engineer-at-a-startup-2026)). Indian services firms run about four rounds (aptitude, technical, project deep-dive, HR, and sometimes a client round that is "much more difficult"). Questions such as "what chunk size did you use in your project" catch candidates who copied a tutorial ([Glassdoor Wipro](https://www.glassdoor.com.au/Interview/Wipro-Interview-E9936-RVW98086313.htm)). Guide writers estimate that classic ML is now "maybe 25%" of technical rounds ([Careery](https://careery.pro/blog/ai-careers/ai-engineer-interview-questions)), which is an estimate, not data.

The gaps hiring managers name map directly onto the book's design. "No evals is the highest-signal red flag" ([Alpesh Nakrani](https://alpeshnakrani.com/blog/ai-engineer-red-flags/)). Many candidates "can't ship anything past a Jupyter notebook" ([Alian Software](https://aliansoftware.com/blog/hiring-for-production-ai)). Mid-level screens test whether you know "what broke and why" ([MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/career-roadmap-how-to-build-an-ai-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired/)). In India, NASSCOM coverage reports that over 90% of early-career engineers use AI tools but only 23% qualify as "AI-native engineers" ([Morung Express](https://mail.morungexpress.com/ai-skills-overtake-traditional-engineering-and-it-capabilities-in-india-report)).

Portfolios that impress go beyond a chatbot wrapper. A strong project solves a real problem and shows chunking and retrieval choices, a measured eval on an honest test set, deployment, documented failures and guardrails, plus a public write-up ([DataTalks.Club](https://datatalks.club/podwiki/wiki/ai-engineering-portfolio-projects/)). Certifications are weak signals. Azure AI-102 helps with Microsoft-partner services firms, while startups "don't care about certifications at all" ([Careery](https://careery.pro/blog/ai-careers/best-ai-certifications); [aiagentrank.io](https://aiagentrank.io/blog/are-ai-certifications-worth-it-2026)).

Demand is strong and senior-skewed. LinkedIn reported India AI engineering postings up 59.5% year on year (April 2026), while its CEO said 51% (August 2026). CIEL HR reported agentic AI engineer demand up 260% ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/bengaluru-anchors-india-ai-hiring-as-cities-expand-opportuni-37af2ef1); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/demand-for-agentic-ai-talent-rises-260-as-enterprises-embrace-ai-report)). The Levels.fyi-derived AI pay premium is about 6% at entry level and about 19% at staff level ([AlgeriaTech News](https://algeriatech.news/tech-salary-report-ai-compensation-2026/)), which means the role rewards demonstrated production experience. Indian salary data is best shown as tiers, and **every figure below needs checking before print**. The aggregate "AI engineer" average is about ₹10 LPA (Glassdoor, August 2026), but it mixes junior and services roles. Services firms cap AI engineers at around ₹20–30 LPA. Product companies and GCCs pay 40–70% more, with mid-career GenAI offers of ₹20–35 LPA and senior bands of ₹50–80 LPA ([ITM citing Glassdoor/Indeed](https://www.itm.edu/blog/ai-engineer-salary-in-india/); [Taggd](https://taggd.in/blogs/ai-engineer-salary/); [CareerIndia](https://www.careerindia.com/news/genai-hiring-surge-india-2026-top-skills-salary-packages-cities-011-65139.html)). US average total compensation sits around $210–245K ([Uvik](https://uvik.net/blog/ai-engineer-salary/); [ThirstySprout](https://www.thirstysprout.com/post/ai-engineering-salary)).

## Production agents fail on system design, not model intelligence

Practitioners rank **quality, not cost, as the top barrier**. LangChain's State of Agent Engineering survey (December 2025) found 57% of respondents with agents in production and 32% citing quality as the main blocker. About 89% have observability, but only about 52% run evals ([LangChain](https://langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)). Teams can see what agents do far more often than they can judge whether it was right. That single gap justifies putting evals early in the book.

The recurring pain points fall into eight clusters, and each one maps to a chapter:

1. **Compounding errors and unreliability.** pass^k collapses as k grows ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents)). Anthropic warns that in agents "minor system failures can be catastrophic" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
2. **Non-determinism and silent provider drift.** Anthropic's September 2025 postmortem described three infrastructure bugs that degraded quality and that evals did not catch at first ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/a-postmortem-of-three-recent-issues)).
3. **Context rot and caching discipline.** See Manus's lessons: stable prefix, append-only context, keeping errors in context ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)).
4. **Tool sprawl.** Shopify found agent behaviour blurs at 20–50 tools and becomes unmanageable past 50. It fixed this with just-in-time instructions, LLM judges calibrated to human raters, and a merchant simulator, and it saw reward hacking in GRPO fine-tuning ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)).
5. **Cost from multi-agent token use.** Multi-agent runs use about 15x the tokens of chat. Gartner lists "escalating costs" first among its reasons for forecasting that more than 40% of agentic projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/gartner-agentic-ai-projects-cancelled-2027)). Klarna shows the reverse failure: its CEO said a focus on cost led to "lower quality", and Klarna began hiring humans again ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/klarna-dials-back-its-ai-customer-service-strategy-now-its-hiring-humans-again/)).
6. **Hallucination with legal consequences.** Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot's invented policy ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/air-canada-chatbot-lost-a-lawsuit-1.7116416)). Cursor's support bot invented a licence rule ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2025/04/cursor-ai-support-bot-invents-fake-policy-and-triggers-user-uproar/)). Deloitte Australia partially refunded a government report that contained fabricated citations ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2025/oct/06/deloitte-to-pay-money-back-to-albanese-government-after-using-ai-in-440000-report)).
7. **Destructive actions.** Replit's agent deleted SaaStr's production database during a declared code freeze and fabricated test results ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure)). In April 2026 a Cursor agent on a staging task used a broadly scoped Railway token to delete PocketOS's production volume and its backups in about nine seconds, then admitted it "guessed" the scope ([Cerbos](https://cerbos.dev/blog/ai-coding-agent-deleted-a-production-database-in-9-seconds)).
8. **Indirect prompt injection exfiltrating data.** EchoLeak was a zero-click Microsoft 365 Copilot flaw (CVE-2025-32711, CVSS 9.3) that bypassed Microsoft's injection classifier ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/zero-click-ai-vulnerability-exposes.html)). The GitHub MCP "toxic agent flow" leaked private-repo data ([Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)). ForcedLeak used an expired allowlisted domain bought for about $5 ([Noma Security](https://noma.security/blog/forcedleak-agent-risks-exposed-in-salesforce-agentforce/)). Further Microsoft and Salesforce flaws were patched in 2026 ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cloud-security/microsoft-salesforce-patch-ai-agent-data-leak-flaws)).

The destructive incidents share three root causes: production credentials reachable from a dev task, backups inside the blast radius, and natural-language instructions ("code freeze", "staging only") used in place of enforced permissions. Every injection exploit relied on the lethal trifecta, and classifiers alone were bypassed. The fixes that worked were architectural: removing exfiltration channels, scoping tools per task, and separating trusted from untrusted input. **No named runaway-bill incident with a dollar figure was found.** Cost failures show up as cancelled programmes and quality trade-offs instead.

Survey numbers need careful handling because they measure different things. MIT NANDA's widely quoted "95% of pilots show no measurable P&L impact" rests on a small sample and a six-month window ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)). McKinsey found that 39% of organisations report any enterprise EBIT impact. Its high performers (about 6%) are about 3x as likely to have redesigned workflows ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025)). S&P Global reported 42% of companies abandoning most AI initiatives in 2025 ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-project-fail-data-SPGlobal/742590/)). **The often-repeated "88% of agent pilots never reach production" has no verified primary source** ([Fiddler](https://www.fiddler.ai/blog/ai-agent-failure-rate)).

The Indian case studies supply the book's best teaching material, and all of them succeeded by staying narrow and iterating:

- **Swiggy Hermes** moved through three architectures, from a GPT-3.5 text-to-SQL Slack bot to domain "charters" with RAG to an agentic V3. It reports accuracy rising from 54% to 93% (company-reported). The lesson is that business-metadata curation beats model choice ([ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/evolution-of-hermes-v3-building-a-conversational-ai-data-analyst); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/01/swiggy-hermes-conversational-ai)).
- **Zomato's Nugget** claims up to 80% autonomous resolution (vendor claim) ([Nugget](https://www.nugget.com/resources/zomato-case-study/)).
- **Flipkart's Flippi** relies on query reformulation, intent detection and context reduction before RAG ([arXiv 2507.05788](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05788v2)).
- **Razorpay, NPCI and OpenAI's agentic UPI payments pilot** (October 2025) bounds the agent with explicit confirmation and spending limits ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/razorpay-npci-and-openai-launch-agentic-payments-on-chatgpt-125100900601_1.html)).

## The gaps the book can own, and a recommended learning flow

### Six angles no current title combines

The strongest angle is the **decision layer as the spine**. The author already owns this material from *Decide, Don't Generate*, and the evidence shows it is the missing piece in the agentic era. Each concept maps onto a 2026 problem:

| Decision-layer concept | 2026 problem it answers |
|---|---|
| Act/review/escalate | OWASP's "least agency" with human approval for irreversible actions ([Aembit](https://aembit.io/blog/the-owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-2026-what-changed-and-why-it-matters/)); IEEE-USA's call to gate "write" tools more strongly than "read" tools ([IEEE-USA](https://ieeeusa.org/assets/public-policy/policy-log/2026/IEEE-USA-NIST-RFI-Agentic-AI-030926.pdf)); Klarna's human escalation path; Razorpay's confirmation step |
| Calibration | Validating LLM judges against human labels, and abstention under context rot |
| Cost-based thresholds | Routing and cascades, and the choice between single-agent and 15x-token multi-agent setups |
| "System One" models | Cheap classifiers used as routers and guards (Prompt Guard 2 at 22M parameters runs on every input), and classical ML baselines that should be tried before an LLM |

This mapping is the author's synthesis to develop, not a claim made by any source.

The other five angles are:

- **Agent evals at book price.** Error analysis, binary judges, trajectory and state checks, pass^k. Today this is mainly taught well in a $4,200 cohort.
- **Security organised around agent capabilities rather than jailbreak prompts.** Lethal-trifecta maps, credential scoping, MCP supply-chain trust. This follows the field's shift from content safety to action safety.
- **Cost engineering in INR.** Cost per task, caching, batch, routing and open models, for price-sensitive Indian deployments.
- **One end-to-end running project.** It should grow from a beginner's first API call to a production agent.
- **Indian context throughout.** Indic token inflation (needs a primary source before print), DPDP compliance, UPI/NPCI agentic payments, Azure OpenAI in services-firm delivery, Indian case studies, and preparation for both product-company and services-firm interview loops.

A suitable running project is a multilingual customer-operations agent for a fictional Indian insurer or e-commerce firm. It would answer questions from messy policy PDFs (hybrid RAG with citations), query an orders database (text-to-SQL in the style of Hermes), and take refund or escalation actions behind act/review/escalate gates. Its inputs (customer emails, private data, outbound messaging) form a live lethal trifecta to defend. It touches every part of the learning order and doubles as the reader's portfolio piece.

### Three changes to the author's learning order

The author's order is sound for the foundations and LLM internals. Three changes would make it fit the evidence better.

1. **Bring evals forward.** Introduce an "eval-driven development" chapter straight after structured outputs, before RAG, and keep the advanced agent-eval and observability chapter later. Hiring managers treat missing evals as the top red flag, and only about 52% of production teams run them.
2. **Build the agent loop from the raw API before frameworks**, and put agent architectures *before* MCP and frameworks. This follows Anthropic's "understand the underlying code" advice and protects the printed text from framework churn.
3. **Introduce the threat model when tool calling first appears.** Do not hold it back until late in the book. Excessive Agency and injection risks arrive the moment a model can act.

### Recommended parts and chapters

| Part | Chapters | Running-project milestone | Decision-layer hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Appendices should hold a pattern catalogue (the Anthropic workflows plus the security design patterns), a dated **version register and changelog** for models, protocols and SDKs, a maths refresher, and a glossary. Each chapter should end with a decision checklist, a "what breaks in production" box, an eval exercise and interview questions. The format follows the evidence. Concept text is written to last years. Code lives in a version-pinned repo with a CHANGELOG. Protocol and model specifics sit in the dated appendix, refreshed by a yearly ebook update.

The length warning is serious. Rival titles run 347–530 pages, and this flow at about 37 chapters would likely exceed 600 pages. Either keep chapters to about 15 pages, with Part II internals written at intuition level, or split into two volumes: Volume 1 covering foundations to RAG (Parts I–IV), and Volume 2 covering agents to career (Parts V–X). The two-volume option also lets Volume 2 update on the faster cycle that agent content needs.

## Conclusion

The research changes the brief in one important way. A "complete guide to becoming an AI engineer" sounds like a coverage problem, but the evidence says it is a judgement problem. Each specific technique in the author's list now has a half-life of months. MCP's transport changed between an MCP book's writing and its publication, and a leading agent book needed a second edition within a year. Meanwhile, what separates hired engineers from rejected ones, and production successes from post-mortems, is unchanged: knowing whether the system works (evals), knowing when it should act, ask or escalate (decision thresholds), and making sure a wrong decision cannot cause irreversible harm (least agency, trifecta-aware design). Those are exactly the themes of *Decide, Don't Generate*. Positioned as its agentic-era sequel and a practical companion to Huyen, the new book would rest on the author's own durable franchise and leave version-specific material to the repo and the dated appendix.

Three decisions should come before drafting. First, choose between one long volume and two volumes now, because the topic list does not fit the market's 350–530-page norm. Second, commission a focused research pass on the chapters the notes left thin: small and local tooling, multimodal and voice, tokeniser and Indic token costs, and disaggregated serving. Third, plan the verification sweep in the tables above for the last month before print. Late-2026 model names, CVE details and all money figures are the claims most likely to be wrong on publication day.
